Settle — three ways to feel safe
Each direction shows the Photos timeline, the viewer and Trash in light and dark. Same library, same moment: Saturday 31 October 2026, 10:24. Use Tweaks → backupState to compare how each one shows "all safe", "needs attention" and "paused".
1a
Ledger
Settle as a well-kept record
Optimises for
Reading status in words. The status is one calm sentence under the title, and day headings read like a journal. Warm paper neutrals flatter skin tones and stay out of the photos' way.
Risks
Serif type and text-only tabs feel less like stock Android. Sentences need careful Turkish copy to stay short. Paper tones can look yellowish next to cool photos.
Why it feels like Settle
The app has a voice. "Nothing was lost" is set in type, not hidden in a toast. Trust comes from what Settle says, not from a wall of badges.
Wallpaper colour
Partly. The moss accent can take the wallpaper's primary; the warm neutrals and the error and warning tones stay fixed on purpose.

1b
Tidepool
Soft, tonal, at home on Android
Optimises for
Comfort and familiarity. Soft tonal surfaces, big rounded targets, and one status pill that changes its tone and its words when something needs you.
Risks
It is the closest to the platform default. Without a well-made pill and motion, it could pass for any Material app. Rounded cells cost a little density.
Why it feels like Settle
The pill is the app's heartbeat: always in the same place, always one sentence. Calm teal when safe, amber when paused, red only when you must act.
Wallpaper colour
Built for it. Every token maps to a tonal role, so a wallpaper seed recolours the whole app. Warning and error colours never follow the wallpaper.

1c
Lightbox
Photos edge to edge; safety stays silent until it matters
Optimises for
Photos, edge to edge. A 1 px grid, floating chrome and a monochrome UI. Backed-up items carry no mark at all, and one signal colour is kept for things you must act on.
Risks
Monochrome can feel cool and technical. The hairline needs a first-run explanation. The icon-only viewer and nav rely on long-press labels and content descriptions.
Why it feels like Settle
The horizon: one hairline under the title that is the backup state. It is solid when safe, dashed when paused and signal-coloured when something needs you. Mono type for facts reads as precise.
Wallpaper colour
Deliberately not. Neutral greys keep photos true to colour. Only the signal colour could optionally follow the wallpaper, as long as it keeps AA contrast.
